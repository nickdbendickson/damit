16 - Find ponds and dugouts (nddam:findponds)

----------------
Description
----------------
Finds the ponds and dugouts among a layer of water bodies and writes them as dams, so the screening tools (5 breach hydrograph, 6 triage, 7 flood simulation, 8 hazard classification, 9, 10) run on them unchanged. This tool takes the place of tools 1-4 for ponds: it writes the dam inventory with the tool-3 storage fields, the stage-storage curves, the breach points and flow arrows, and a terrain clip for every pond that can breach. Use a separate work folder from the DWR dam run.
INPUT: water-body polygons - NHD waterbodies (the data package of tool 0 has them), NWI wetlands, or your own layer - and the LiDAR terrain (USGS 3DEP or your local DEM tiles). The tool does not download water bodies: load the layer in QGIS and pick it here.
HOW IT DECIDES (core/ponds.py). The pool is the flat water surface of the DEM, else the shoreline. The ground around the water is cut into 16 compass sectors; in each, the RIM is the highest ground within the 'crest search distance' of the water and the TOE the foot of the embankment face: where the ground below the rim stops falling steeper than your 'face' angle (natural slopes are gentler), searched out to the 'toe search distance' (feet). Where the pool is higher than the toe by at least your 'head' value the sector holds water above the ground. An arc of at least your 'minimum arc' = EMBANKMENT POND: the crest (top of dam) is the lowest rim of those sectors, the natural ground the breach cuts down to is their lowest toe, and the breach point is the rim of the sector with the lowest toe, flow away from the water. Otherwise the water sits in a hole: a DUGOUT when the banks are steep, a raised spoil rim surrounds it or the layer's own type field says excavated, else a NATURAL DEPRESSION.
DUGOUTS RELEASE ONLY WHAT IS ABOVE THE GROUND: the volume between the natural ground and the lowest rim (a spoil rim can hold some). Below your 'smallest breach flood' volume - or when the rim is lower than the 0.3 m breach height tool 5 can route - the pond is marked no_flood = YES, no flood is simulated, and tool 8 reports 'Low - no breach flood'.
STORAGE: above the pool from the DEM, below the pool by the shoreline-slope proxy of tool 3 (the LiDAR cannot see under the water; the greatest depth is YOUR assumption). No inventory storage exists for ponds, so stor_method = estimated.
DWR dugouts (optional, 0.17.54; filled from the suite folder): a water body that contains a DWR dugout point or polygon, or whose inside point is in a DWR dugout polygon or within the buffer (default 100 ft = 30 m) of a DWR dugout point, gets dwr_dugout = that structure_id and is classed a dugout whatever its LiDAR shape: only the water above the natural ground is released. Empty = no change. EVERY THRESHOLD IS YOUR VALUE: the starting values are not standards. Natural depressions (prairie potholes) are left out of the dam layer unless you ask for them and are listed in the excluded-water-bodies layer with the reason. Check the classes on a few ponds against an aerial image before running a whole county. Screening only: the LiDAR is not a survey, small ponds are rarely hydro-flattened, and the official classification is the Department's. Not run in a real QGIS.
