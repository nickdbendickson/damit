description=Finds dams, dry dams, road fills, ponds, dugouts and road / rail stream crossings in LiDAR, hydrography and aerial imagery that are not on the NID, state or bridge / culvert lists you give.
about=Nine Processing tools (Processing Toolbox > DamIT Finder > Find structures). Fills across streams and draws: walks the 3DHP / NHD flowlines and every draw traced from the DEM itself over a bare-earth DEM and reports every fill that blocks the valley (spill level, height, head, storage, pool), as a dam, dry embankment or road fill. Ponds and dugouts: classes every polygon of a water-body layer from the LiDAR. Crossings: every road / rail x stream crossing, the places a culvert or bridge must be. Open water: pond outlines from 4-band or colour-infrared aerial imagery (NDWI; several years keep the persistent water) or straight from a classified LiDAR point cloud (ASPRS class 9), marked when no water-body layer has them. Build the water mosaic: turn a folder of classified LiDAR tiles into the class-9 water-count / mean-elevation rasters (CONUS Albers, the whole U.S.) the open-water tool reads. Also: where each pool spills, dug pits and raised ridges (levees, dikes) anywhere in the DEM, the drainage area and expected pipe size at each crossing, dams / dikes / culverts from OpenStreetMap, a score learned from the fills already on a dam list (four kinds, bagged boosted trees among them), evidence the DEM cannot give (water year after year in the imagery, NWI codes, LiDAR return drop-out, buildings and roads down the flow path), and review batches whose marks give precision and recall with honest 95 %% intervals. Every candidate is checked against the known-structure layers you give (NID, state lists, NBI, DOT culverts, USGS / SARP crossings): listed, unsure or new. Runs no flood model. Candidates for review against an aerial image, not determinations; a new dam has no NID ID, so DamIT Screener does not take it as a dam record until it is listed.

changelog=
    0.6.0 - the Iowa misses. Lines out of each pond (POOLS, on with the draws; POOL_MAX_AC, 50 ac): every flat water surface and closed depression in the DEM gets a short line out through its outlet down the opened 10 m routing, and only the pool's own fill is tested on it (line_source 'DEM pool outlet'), so the dams of ponds that drain too little for the draws, or that a draw crosses at a low spot, are found. Brim-full ponds (full to within 0.15 m of the spill) are tested again from the pool's lowest ground and kept when the pool gets out at the fill and drains under 200 ac (field rescued). drain_ac on a pool line reads the pond's own ground, never a creek beside it or one leaving by a culvert elsewhere. Measured on 15 Iowa 1 m tiles against the NRCS 378 pond-dam census: a fill within 50 m of 929 of 1,213 pond dams (77 %%; 0.5: 41 %%), ranked with AUC 0.89 as before; in North Dakota (66 tiles) listed-dam recall 67 %% to 71 %% with the top of each tile holding as many listed dams. Score model unchanged.
    0.5.0 - the small-dam research build order. Each fill carries where its pool spills (outlet_dist_ft, spill_on_fill, drain_ac, drain_ratio), one fill per pool (same_pool), the head above the original ground put back under the fill (ag_head_ft) and the share of the pool below it (below_frac: about 1 for a dug pit), the pool's and fill's shape and the terrain round the crest; the toe walk stops at the foot of the face on a steep draw. Score tool: four kinds (v1, 0.5, 0.5 terrain, 0.5 trees = bagged PU boosted trees) and reviewers' marks as trusted labels, with score_note when a fill is scored by extrapolation; a listed dam whose list says it is breached, removed, not built or submerged (a status field) is left out of training and of the checks, and every candidate carries known_state. On North Dakota's tests the 0.5 trees kind ranked a reviewer's 31 marked dams among 245 cards with AUC 0.89 against 0.63 for the 0.4 score, and each region held out scored 0.85-0.93; learned only from North Dakota, it ranked 15 Iowa LiDAR tiles against Iowa's complete pond-dam census with AUC 0.89 against 0.84. New tool damit_find:evidence (water year after year from NAIP colour-infrared and 4-band imagery, bare ground, NWI h / x and water regime, quarries, soils, LiDAR return drop-out in the pool read only where a survey reached, buildings and roads down the flow path within 1 / 3 / 5 miles, a storage permit check at a threshold you set) and damit_find:review_batch (a fixed random control, top-ranked and near-the-cut cards of the cards not yet reviewed, each with its chance of being picked, and a share for a blind second reviewer); core/evaluate.py turns marks into precision at k and the share of real dams in the list with honest 95 %% intervals (Wilson, Horvitz-Thompson, DeLong, paired bootstrap); core/dataset.py files each reviewed card with its aerial and hillshade chips. The water-mosaic tool can also write the count of all LiDAR returns per cell.
    0.4.4 - Find ponds and dugouts can take the class-9 water-surface mosaic (water_elev.vrt from 'Build a class-9 water mosaic', inputs WATER_ELEV + band) and read each pond's pool elevation straight from the LiDAR water surface instead of inferring it from the DEM (flat surface or shoreline); the output pool_method says 'measured LiDAR water surface' when used. Also: the fill classifier no longer calls a fill a 'dam' on the strength of a flat water surface alone - a dam holds water above the downstream ground (upstream level clears the toe by more than the valley's own fall), so an excavated dugout or gravel pit (standing water, no fill holding it up) is now 'dry embankment', not 'dam'. (A Wells County blind review of 89 unlisted candidates showed geometry alone cannot separate small stock dams from dugouts / approaches - same head, pool, height - so no size / storage threshold was added; the fill-score ranking stays the prioritiser.) 0.4.2 - new optional tool 'Build a class-9 water mosaic from LiDAR tiles' (damit_find:water_mosaic): turns a folder of classified LiDAR tiles (.las/.laz) into the water_count.vrt and water_elev.vrt that 'Find open water' reads with its WATER_RASTER input - the scalable path (build once, then the open-water tool reads the mosaic a spatial tile at a time; the raw statewide point cloud is terabytes). Per tile a water-count GeoTIFF (class-9 points per cell) and a mean-elevation GeoTIFF (the pond water surface); every tile snaps to one global grid so the VRT has no seams; dry land and the gaps between tiles read back as no water. Output CRS is CONUS Albers (EPSG:5070) for the whole U.S. (the DamSafety build uses North Dakota's UTM 14N). Reuses core/lidar, launches no outside process; verified end to end (a real compressed LAZ -> the mosaic -> the open-water tool finds the pond). 0.4.1 - the open-water tool can read water straight from a classified LiDAR point cloud (.las/.laz) instead of aerial imagery: a cell is water when the cloud classes it so (ASPRS class 9), so there is no NDWI threshold, sun angle, shadow or imagery year to guess at - the classification is already made (USGS 3DEP delivers classified LAZ). Give one tile (LAS) or a folder (LAS_DIR); LAS_CLASSES (water classes, default 9), LAS_EPSG (tile CRS only when the file carries none), LAS_MIN_PTS. core/lidar.py reads with laspy if installed (LAS and LAZ), else a small pure-numpy reader for uncompressed LAS (so the class filter and rasteriser are unit-tested without laspy or GDAL); it launches no outside process. It can also read a prebuilt class-9 water-count raster / VRT (WATER_RASTER): a cell is water where the count is at least WATER_COUNT_MIN - the scalable path (feed the statewide class-9 water mosaic) when reading the raw LiDAR statewide is too much. 0.4.0 - three Wells-review follow-ups: finds a dike that holds water running beside a stream or draw (a dam built along a channel, not across it; on by default); the downstream toe carries past a bench on the face to the outlet channel, so the height is read at the foot not off a shelf (Absaraka 27.4 ft vs 27.5 listed, was ~21); the open-water tool drops cast shadows (a green-brightness test) and, with a buildings/streets layer, roofs, pavement and in-town pools. 0.3.0 - a fill on no road / rail layer whose raised ground runs on 600 m or more is a new kind, long fill (road, rail, canal or levee), not a dam (Wells County aerial review, 2026-11-12: canal banks and a divided highway missing from the road layers were the top "dams"); ridge_len_ft column and score input (a 0.2 model still scores). 0.2.0 - first release (versioned with the DamIT suite): damit_find:embankments, damit_find:ponds, damit_find:crossings, damit_find:water (open water in 4-band / colour-infrared aerial imagery, one or several years), damit_find:osm (dams / dikes / culverts from OpenStreetMap), damit_find:score (a fill score learned from listed dams); fills on draws traced from the DEM, spillways, dug pits and raised ridges, NWI codes and dugout shape, drainage area and pipe size at crossings; listed / unsure / new against the known-structure layers; review hillshade. Review fixes (2026-11-11): a DEM recording no elevation unit is refused until Metres or Feet is chosen (USGS 3DEP files = metres; warning when the ground is outside ND); Cancel stops within the tile and fails with 'Cancelled' (crossings too); screening_dams holds no road fill; a run_info table (tool, version, GDAL, time, settings) in each output; shorter check-box labels; help text names no DamSafety plugin.
